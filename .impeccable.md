# Design Context — Shrinkray

## Target audience
Anyone who just got "file too large" when trying to send a video: gamers posting clips to Discord (10 MB free limit),
parents emailing a recital clip (25 MB Gmail limit), students submitting to a portal, people sending to WhatsApp/iMessage,
small-business owners uploading to a form. Mostly non-technical, on a laptop or phone, in a hurry, slightly annoyed.
Secondary: privacy-conscious people who refuse to upload personal video to random "free online compressor" sites.

## Use cases
1. "Make this video under 10 MB / 25 MB so I can send it" — target size is the job, not codec settings.
2. Quick trim + shrink of a phone video before sharing.
3. Batch-shrink a few clips.
All of it must work with zero uploading and zero sign-up; the file never leaves the device.

## Brand personality / tone
A precision tool with a wink: a "shrink ray" from a pulp sci-fi comic, but executed with lab-instrument restraint.
Confident, fast, plain-spoken. Delight comes from speed and honesty ("312 MB → 9.6 MB in 41 s"), not from decoration.
Warm light theme (paper + ink + one hot accent), not another dark neon dev tool. Typography-led; no stock icons.

## Constraints
Static site (GitHub Pages), vanilla HTML/CSS/JS, no build step, one vendored library (mediabunny). Must be excellent
on mobile Safari and Chrome. Accessible (keyboard, contrast, reduced motion). Works offline once loaded.
